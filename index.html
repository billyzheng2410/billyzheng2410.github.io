Jinghan Zheng

I am an Honours Computer Science undergraduate at McGill University, interested in how language models use context—and how to make them more reliable.

My research spans contextual robustness, in-context learning, and mechanistic interpretability. I study attention-head interventions in language models and claim-level auditing for retrieval-augmented generation. I also work on multi-task learning for agricultural computer vision.

Researcher · First Author

2026–present

Contextual Entrainment in Language Models

McGill University

I investigate whether interventions that reduce contextual entrainment are specific to individual relations or transfer across them. I built a benchmark covering 19 lexical-semantic subtypes, used differentiable head search and causal ablation in LLaMA-3.1-8B, and replicated the main transfer pattern on Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-7B.

Independent Research · First Author

OR-Wolf: Claim-Level Auditing for Reliable Multi-Agent RAG

I developed a framework that audits atomic claims against reference evidence and balances incorrect answers, false abstentions, deletion of supported evidence, and audit cost. Experiments on SciFact compare answer voting, agent filtering, and claim auditing using three Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct agents and a DeBERTa-v3 verifier.

Research Assistant

2026–present

Agricultural Multi-Task Vision Foundation Model

Nanjing Agricultural University & Huazhong University of Science and Technology · Remote

Supervisors: Prof. Shouyang Liu and Prof. Hao Lu

I develop a DINOv3-based model for wheat disease classification, wheat-head detection, and organ segmentation. My work combines shared and task-specific low-rank adapters with separate prediction heads, and examines how feature-distribution alignment affects convergence and performance across tasks.

Shared Reality Lab, McGill University
